Privacy policy for the ink puzzle.

InkBleedHazard is a free casual game. You tap a limited number of times, drop ink on a parchment shape, and try to fill it before the bleed crosses the safe zone. This policy explains that product’s privacy posture for players and families. It is a plain-language summary, not legal advice for your country.

Not collected The App Store label says the developer does not collect data from this app.
No profile A level starts from a drop, not from a login, name, or contact list.
Age 4+ The store age rating is 4+. A guardian chooses the install.
01

Scope

This policy covers the InkBleedHazard app distributed on the App Store and the two pages published with it: the game introduction and this policy. It describes the publisher of the game as a product, and it does not point to a personal homepage.

Apple’s own services — the store, your Apple ID, system backups, and parental controls — follow Apple’s terms and Apple’s privacy policy. Those services are named here only so the boundary is clear.

02

Play without a profile

Opening a level does not ask for an account. The puzzle does not request an email address, phone number, photo, contact book, precise location, or a public player name before the first drop.

The actions that matter in InkBleedHazard are on-screen: choosing a point on the parchment, spending one tap from the level’s limit, watching ink pool through rivers, mazes, bottlenecks, and friction zones, then either filling the shape or ruining the sheet. None of those gestures is a form you submit to a server described in the store listing.

03

Store privacy label

On the App Store, the privacy section for InkBleedHazard states that data is not collected. The developer indicated that the app does not collect data. This page does not add advertising identifiers, analytics events, or partner SDKs that the listing does not show.

Apple notes that privacy practices may vary, for example with the features you use or with your age. If the live label on the product page changes, that label is the source to re-check. This document will be replaced when the described practice changes.

A “data not collected” label is the opposite of a hidden event log. This policy does not invent one.

04

Level session data

A run has ordinary puzzle state: which of the ten levels you are on, how many taps remain, whether ink stayed inside the safe zone, and how many stars the run produced. That state belongs to the installed game on the iPhone, iPad, or other Apple device where you play.

The store label does not describe that state as data collected by the developer. Deleting InkBleedHazard removes the local app copy the system kept for it. If you have turned on a device backup yourself, such as iCloud Backup, Apple may include app data under the backup settings on your Apple ID. There is no separate InkBleedHazard cloud account in the product description.

05

Stars and revives

Stars are an in-game tally from how cleanly a shape is filled. The game lets you spend them on a revive in the middle of a level, so a failing drop does not have to end the sheet immediately. That exchange is a rule of the puzzle.

The App Store lists the app as free. This policy does not describe a wallet, a saved payment card, or a publisher account used to buy stars. Any purchase flow Apple itself presents at download time, if one appears on your device, is Apple’s transaction.

06

Apple distribution

InkBleedHazard is obtained from the App Store. Download, update, refund, and the privacy switches in iOS and iPadOS are handled by Apple. The game requires iOS 15 or later and is offered in English. The listing presents it for iPhone and iPad, and it is designed for iPad.

Information Apple already holds because you use an Apple ID is governed by Apple. Installing this puzzle does not move that information into a publisher profile.

07

These pages

The introduction page and this policy are static pages about InkBleedHazard. They do not include an account, a newsletter, a comment box, or a payment form. Type is set with fonts already on your device, so opening either page is not a request to a separate font host.

The outbound link on both pages is the App Store listing for this game. There is no personal website attached to the pages.

08

Younger players

The age rating on the store is 4+. A parent or guardian decides whether the puzzle is installed. Screen Time, Ask to Buy, and Family Sharing can block or approve that install where those Apple controls are turned on.

The game does not begin by asking a child to register. If you believe a child sent personal information to the publisher outside ordinary play, use the support options on the App Store listing and include only what is needed to identify the request.

09

Requests

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, or delete personal information, or to object to certain processing. Because the store label states that data is not collected from the app, the publisher may have no InkBleedHazard file to produce. You can still ask.

Send the request through the support options on the InkBleedHazard App Store listing. This site does not publish a personal email address. Please do not include identity documents unless a reply specifically asks for a narrower detail.

  • Say that the request is about InkBleedHazard privacy.
  • Describe what you want done.
  • Keep unrelated personal details out of the first message.
10

Updates

The effective date above is the date of this wording. If a later build of InkBleedHazard starts collecting data, this page will be rewritten and the App Store privacy label should be updated to match. Until then, the practice described here is on-device ink play under a store label of data not collected.

The listing, the privacy label, and support for InkBleedHazard are on the App Store page.
