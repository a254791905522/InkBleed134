Drop the ink.Don’t ruin the page.

InkBleedHazard is a tactical fluid puzzle on parchment. You place a strict number of drops, then the ink pools and spreads on its own. Fill the shape. If the bleed crosses the safe zone, the sheet is ruined and the level is lost.

Inside the line Bleed hazard

Place

Tap the parchment to set a drop. The spot you choose is the whole decision.

Spread

Ink pools and creeps outward. You guide the flow by where it starts, not by painting the shape.

Friction

Sticky zones drag the bleed and kill momentum before a gap is full.

Tap limit

Each level allows only so many drops. Extra taps are not a backup plan.

Safe zone

Cross the boundary and the page is ruined. The level ends on that overflow.

Precision first. Patience second.

The sheet does not give you a brush. It gives you a budget of drops and a shape that must fill itself. Read the chokepoints before the first tap.

  1. 01

    Read the vessel

    Ten levels use winding rivers, grid mazes, tight chokepoints, and internal traps. The outline is the safe zone.

  2. 02

    Spend a tap

    Set the drop where the spread can reach a chamber without rushing the edge. One poorly placed tap can finish the sheet early.

  3. 03

    Watch the friction

    Heavy sticky zones slow the ink. A drop that looks close may stall, while an open lane can overshoot the boundary.

  4. 04

    Fill, or lose the page

    Coverage inside the line clears the level. Ink past the safe zone ruins the parchment and the run stops.

Ten shapes. Three kinds of trouble.

Every level is a different vessel. The shared rule is the same: calculate the drop, respect the tap count, and keep the bleed on the page.

Rivers

Long channels carry ink farther than a single glance suggests. A late bend is where overflow starts.

Mazes

Grid paths split one drop into competing lanes. Internal traps punish a drop set in the wrong cell.

Bottlenecks

Sharp chokepoints leave almost no margin. The safe zone is close on both sides of the pinch.

Sticky parchment

Friction changes the clock.

Some regions aggressively slow the flow. You still cannot add taps to push through them. The remedy is a better first drop, or a revive bought with stars if the run is still salvageable.

Stars buy a second chance mid-level.

Cleaner fills earn stars. Those stars are spent inside the puzzle on a revive when a drop is about to ruin the sheet. They are a score you play for, described in the game as the way to purchase that mid-level rescue. The App Store lists InkBleedHazard as a free casual game.
